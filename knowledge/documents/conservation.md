# Groundwater Conservation and Management

Sustainable groundwater management aims to balance extraction with recharge so that aquifers continue to supply water for future generations.

## Demand-side measures

- Efficient irrigation: drip and sprinkler systems, mulching and improved scheduling reduce water applied per crop.
- Crop choice: shifting to less water-intensive crops in water-stressed areas reduces irrigation demand.
- Regulated extraction: metering, limits on new wells and rationing in over-exploited areas.
- Domestic efficiency: fixing leaks, water-efficient fixtures and reuse of grey water.

## Supply-side measures (artificial recharge)

- Percolation tanks and farm ponds that hold monsoon runoff so it can infiltrate.
- Check dams and gully plugs that slow stream flow and encourage infiltration.
- Recharge wells and borewell recharge shafts that direct roof and surface runoff into the aquifer.
- Watershed treatment that improves soil moisture and reduces runoff.

## Participatory groundwater management

Community-based approaches treat groundwater as a shared common-pool resource. Villages monitor water levels, agree on cropping plans and water budgets, and manage recharge structures collectively. Participatory management is most effective when farmers trust the data and understand the connection between their pumping and the regional water level.

## Data and monitoring

Good management depends on good data: water level monitoring networks, extraction estimates, rainfall data and periodic resource assessment. Publishing this data openly, with clear provenance, helps citizens and agencies make informed decisions.
