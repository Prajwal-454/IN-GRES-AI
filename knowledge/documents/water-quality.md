# Groundwater Quality

Groundwater quality refers to the chemical, physical and biological characteristics of water in aquifers. Quality determines whether water is fit for drinking, irrigation or industry.

## Common parameters

- Total Dissolved Solids (TDS): a measure of dissolved salts. High TDS affects taste and can damage crops.
- Electrical Conductivity (EC): closely related to TDS; higher EC means more dissolved salts.
- Hardness: caused mainly by calcium and magnesium. Hard water forms scale and consumes more soap.
- Fluoride: present in some hard-rock aquifers; above the drinking guideline it can cause dental and skeletal fluorosis.
- Arsenic: occurs naturally in some deltaic sediments; chronic exposure is a serious health risk.
- Nitrate: often from fertilizers, animal waste or sewage; high nitrate is harmful to infants.
- Iron and manganese: common in alluvial and coastal aquifers; cause staining and taste issues.
- pH: a measure of acidity or alkalinity; most groundwater is near neutral.

## Sources of contamination

Groundwater contamination can come from geogenic (natural) sources such as arsenic and fluoride in the aquifer matrix, or from anthropogenic sources such as agricultural fertilizers and pesticides, industrial effluent, septic tanks and unlined landfills, and saline intrusion in coastal aquifers where over-extraction draws seawater into the freshwater zone.

## Water level and quality interplay

Over-extraction lowers water levels and can change quality: shallow wells dry up, pumping deeper water may bring up higher salinity, and coastal aquifers may suffer seawater intrusion.
