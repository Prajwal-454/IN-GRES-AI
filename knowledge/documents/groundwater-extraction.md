# Groundwater Extraction

Groundwater extraction is the withdrawal of water from an aquifer, usually through wells, tubewells, borewells and hand pumps. It is also called groundwater abstraction or draft.

## Main uses

In India the dominant use of groundwater is irrigation. Agriculture accounts for the largest share of extraction, followed by domestic use in rural and urban areas and industrial use. The same aquifer often serves all three sectors, so the total extraction is compared against the extractable resource to judge how stressed the aquifer is.

## Extraction vs extraction intensity

Two different numbers are often confused:

- Total annual extraction is the volume of water withdrawn in a year.
- Stage of groundwater extraction is the ratio of annual extraction to the annual extractable groundwater resource, expressed as a percentage. It is the standard indicator used to classify assessment units.

For example, if the annual extractable resource is 100 units and the annual extraction is 75 units, the stage of extraction is 75 percent.

## Estimating extraction

Extraction is not metered in most wells. It is estimated using norms:

- Irrigation extraction is estimated from the area irrigated by groundwater and an irrigation water use norm per unit area.
- Domestic and industrial extraction is estimated from population and per-capita use norms, or from metered supply in larger towns.

These estimates are refined over time as cropping patterns, irrigation practices and metering improve.

## Sustainable extraction

Sustainable extraction keeps annual withdrawals below the annual extractable resource so that storage is not depleted. When extraction persistently exceeds the extractable resource, water levels decline, pumping costs rise, and saline or contaminated water may intrude. Managing extraction through regulation, metering and conjunctive use of surface water is central to groundwater management.
