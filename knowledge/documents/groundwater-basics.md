# Groundwater Basics

Groundwater is the water present beneath Earth's surface in soil pore spaces and in the fractures of rock formations. A unit of rock or an unconsolidated deposit is called an aquifer when it can yield a usable quantity of water.

Groundwater is an important source of freshwater in India, supporting drinking water supply, agriculture and industry. Roughly 85 percent of rural drinking water in India is sourced from groundwater. Because groundwater moves slowly and is hidden underground, it is relatively protected from surface contamination but is difficult to observe and manage.

The quantity of groundwater in a region is described by its groundwater resource. The total annual groundwater recharge is the amount of water added to the aquifer from rainfall and other sources such as canal seepage and return flows from irrigation. The annual extractable groundwater resource is the portion of that recharge that is available for extraction without endangering the aquifer.

Water levels in wells are the direct observable signal of groundwater storage. When extraction exceeds recharge over a long period, water levels decline, wells go dry and aquifers become stressed. This condition is known as groundwater depletion or over-exploitation.

## How groundwater is estimated

Groundwater resource estimation is typically carried out by assessment units. An assessment unit is the smallest geographic unit for which groundwater is assessed, for example a block, a mandal or a taluk. For each assessment unit, agencies estimate the annual recharge, the annual extractable resource and the annual extraction for different uses.

Estimates are based on the groundwater level fluctuation method and the rainfall infiltration method, and are refined using norms for irrigation, domestic and industrial use. Assessments are reviewed periodically so that changes in rainfall, land use and irrigation practice are captured.

## Data trust

Numbers reported by groundwater information systems must always show their source, year and assessment method. Synthetic or demonstration datasets must be clearly labelled so they are never mistaken for official government statistics.
