# Groundwater Recharge

Groundwater recharge is the process by which water moves downward from the land surface into the groundwater system. It is the natural input to the aquifer water balance.

## Sources of recharge

The main source of recharge is rainfall. When rain falls on permeable land, part of it infiltrates the soil and percolates down to the water table. Recharge also occurs from:

- Seepage from canals and irrigation canals
- Return flows from irrigated fields
- Seepage from tanks, ponds and reservoirs
- Infiltration from rivers and streams (indirect recharge)

The amount of recharge depends on rainfall amount and intensity, soil type, geology, slope, land cover and the depth to the water table.

## Rainfall infiltration factor

In groundwater resource estimation, recharge from rainfall is often computed as a percentage of the monsoon rainfall using a rainfall infiltration factor (RIF). The factor varies by geology: hard rocks such as granites and basalts have lower infiltration factors than alluvial plains. Deeper water tables generally allow more infiltration because there is more unsaturated storage available.

## Measuring recharge

Recharge is not measured directly at large scale. Instead, it is estimated using:

1. The groundwater level fluctuation method, which uses the seasonal rise in water levels and the specific yield of the aquifer.
2. The rainfall infiltration method, which applies an infiltration factor to the monsoon rainfall.
3. Water budgeting and modelling for detailed studies.

## Why recharge matters

Recharge sustains the annual extractable groundwater resource. If recharge declines because of erratic rainfall, land-use change or reduced infiltration, the amount of groundwater that can be sustainably extracted also declines. Artificial recharge structures such as recharge pits, percolation tanks and check dams are built to increase recharge in water-stressed areas.
