# Aquifers

An aquifer is a saturated layer of rock or sediment that can store water and transmit it in usable quantities to wells and springs. The word comes from the Latin for water-bearing.

## Types of aquifer

- Unconfined aquifer: a permeable layer open to the surface, where the water table forms the top of the saturated zone. Recharge happens directly from rainfall percolating down.
- Confined aquifer: a permeable layer sandwiched between two low-permeability layers (aquitards). The water is under pressure; a well drilled into a confined aquifer may rise above the aquifer top (artesian conditions).
- Aquitard: a layer that slows water movement, such as clay, but does not completely block it.
- Aquiclude: a nearly impermeable layer, such as dense clay or unfractured rock.

## Aquifer characteristics

- Porosity is the fraction of void space in the rock that can hold water.
- Permeability (hydraulic conductivity) is how easily water moves through the rock.
- Specific yield is the fraction of water that drains from a saturated rock under gravity; it determines how much the water level changes for a given change in storage.
- Transmissivity is the rate at which water is transmitted through the full saturated thickness of the aquifer.

## Aquifers in India

India has a wide range of aquifer systems. Hard rock aquifers in granites and basalts hold water in fractures and weathered zones, with limited storage and high variability between wells. Alluvial aquifers in river plains have better storage and yield. Karst and limestone aquifers can have conduit flow and higher productivity. Managing each type requires different approaches: artificial recharge works well in weathered hard rock, while alluvial aquifers benefit from conjunctive use with surface water.

## Why aquifers matter for assessment

Groundwater is stored and transmitted through aquifers, so the physical properties of the aquifer control how much recharge can be stored and how much extraction is sustainable. Assessment units are defined to be hydrogeologically similar so that estimates are meaningful within each unit.
