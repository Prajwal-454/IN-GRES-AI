# Stage of Groundwater Extraction and Assessment Categories

The stage of groundwater extraction (SoE) is the ratio of annual groundwater extraction to the annual extractable groundwater resource, expressed as a percentage. It is the primary indicator used to assess whether an assessment unit is being over-used.

    Stage of extraction (%) = (Annual extraction / Annual extractable resource) x 100

## GEC-2015 assessment categories

Based on the stage of extraction and long-term water level trends, assessment units are classified into four categories. The widely used thresholds (per the GEC 2015 methodology) are:

- Safe: stage of extraction less than 70 percent, and no significant long-term decline in water level.
- Semi-critical: stage of extraction between 70 and 90 percent, or a small long-term decline in water level.
- Critical: stage of extraction between 90 and 100 percent, or a significant long-term decline in water level.
- Over-exploited: stage of extraction greater than 100 percent, indicating that extraction exceeds the annual extractable resource.

## How categories are used

Category classification drives management priority:

- Safe units are monitored and maintained.
- Semi-critical and critical units need regulation and measures to improve recharge.
- Over-exploited units are highest priority and require demand management, rationing of new connections and active recharge programmes.

## Interpretation notes

A stage of extraction near or above 100 percent does not mean the wells are physically dry; it means that at current extraction the aquifer is being drawn down and is not sustainable. Water level trend data over several years provides additional evidence, so category decisions are not based on a single year alone.

Categories are per assessment unit. A state or district total is an aggregation of its assessment units, not a single measurement.
